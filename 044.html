em drive

FRINGE SCIENCE · reactionless thrusters — a sealed metal cone, filled with microwaves, said to push itself along without throwing anything out the back

Every rocket works by throwing something backwards. In 2001 the British aerospace engineer Roger Shawyer proposed a drive that needs no propellant at all: microwaves bouncing inside a closed, tapered copper cavity. Over the next fifteen years laboratories in Britain, China, the United States and Germany built versions and measured thrust, including a NASA team whose results passed peer review. In 2018 DARPA funded a physicist whose new theory of inertia predicts the effect. If it is real, it would open the solar system. Here you can tune the cavity, read NASA’s thrust balance, and test the theory that explains it.

Light bouncing in a cone

Shawyer’s design factor—

Light carries momentum and pushes on whatever reflects it (Φ 034). Inside the EmDrive, microwaves bounce back and forth between a wide end and a narrow end thousands of times before they fade; the number of round trips is set by the cavity’s quality factor, Q.

Shawyer pointed out that in a tapered waveguide the waves travel at different group velocities near each end. In his analysis, using special relativity, the radiation pressure on the two ends then differs, and the difference is multiplied by every round trip.

The case forThe group velocity of waves in a tapered guide genuinely varies along its length, a standard result of microwave engineering. Shawyer, who spent decades on satellite systems at Astrium, built working prototypes with UK government and industry funding and reported thrust growing with Q as his formula predicts. Because each photon is reused thousands of times, even a tiny imbalance per bounce can add up to a measurable force.
The case againstPeer-reviewed analyses find that once the side walls are included, the forces on a closed cavity cancel exactly under standard electromagnetism.
Try thisRaise the quality factor and the taper and watch the predicted thrust grow. Compare it with a photon rocket, the best propellant-free drive standard physics allows.
The math
T = 2 P Q Df / c

Shawyer’s thrust equation, with P the microwave power and Df a design factor between 0 and 1 set by the difference in group velocity between the two ends. A photon rocket gives P/c, about 3.3 micronewtons per kilowatt.

The claimed forces are tiny, about a thousandth of a newton per kilowatt, so they are measured on a torsion pendulum: a beam on a twisting fibre that swings slightly when pushed, read by a laser. Calibration pulses of known force are added before and after each run.

Harold White’s Eagleworks team at NASA’s Johnson Space Center ran tests in a hard vacuum, at several power levels, and in three orientations.

The case forPublished in 2016 in the AIAA Journal of Propulsion and Power after peer review, the team reported 1.2 ± 0.1 millinewtons per kilowatt in vacuum, about 360 times a photon rocket. The force reversed when the thruster was turned around and largely disappeared in the null orientation, the pattern expected of real thrust rather than a fixed artefact. Earlier experiments in China at Northwestern Polytechnical University and in the US had also reported thrust.
The case againstIn 2021 Martin Tajmar’s group in Dresden, with improved shielding and contacts, traced their own signals to heating and cable effects and found no thrust at a thousand times lower levels.

03Quantised inertia, 2015–

A theory that predicts it

The physicist Mike McCulloch, then at the University of Plymouth, developed a theory he calls quantised inertia. It proposes that inertia comes from a faint radiation every accelerating object sees, the Unruh radiation, and that only wavelengths fitting inside the object’s horizon or container count.

Applied to the EmDrive, more of these waves fit at the wide end than at the narrow end, so photons are slightly heavier at the wide end. To conserve momentum, the cavity must move.

The case forMcCulloch published predictions for EmDrive experiments in EPL in 2015 and reported that his formula matched several measured thrusts within the experimental spread, with no adjustable parameters. The same theory also claims to explain galaxy rotation without dark matter. In 2018 DARPA awarded his group $1.3 million to test quantised inertia in the laboratory.
The case againstQuantised inertia is not accepted by most physicists, and if the best experiments show no thrust there is nothing for it to explain.
Try thisNarrow the small end and lengthen the cavity, and watch the predicted thrust change. Make the two ends equal: the prediction drops to zero.

F ≈ (P Q L / c) × (1/wsmall − 1/wbig)

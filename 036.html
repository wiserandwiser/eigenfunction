the higgs field

PARTICLES & FORCES · the origin of mass — an invisible field fills all of space, and how strongly a particle feels it sets its mass

Without the Higgs field, electrons would be massless and fly at the speed of light, atoms could not form, and the weak force would be as long-ranged as electromagnetism. The field switched on a trillionth of a second after the Big Bang. In 2012, after a 48-year search, its ripple, the Higgs boson, was finally caught.

01Breaking the symmetry

A ball in a Mexican hat

Shape of the energy—
Field value in empty space—
Symmetry—

A field has energy, and the field settles wherever its energy is lowest. For most fields that is zero: empty space contains no electric field, for example. The Higgs field is different. Its energy curve is shaped like a Mexican hat, highest in the middle, so the lowest-energy state of empty space is a trough where the field is not zero.

In the first instants after the Big Bang it was hot enough that the hat was a simple bowl and the field sat at zero. About 10⁻¹² seconds in, the universe cooled enough for the hat to form. The field rolled off the peak into the trough and has stayed there ever since, with a value of about 246 GeV everywhere in the universe.

The hat is perfectly round, but the ball had to land at one particular point on the rim. The laws are symmetric; the state of the universe is not. This is spontaneous symmetry breaking, like a pencil balanced on its tip that must fall some way.

02Mass from the field

Turn the Higgs field up or down

Electron mass—
Hydrogen atom size—
Proton mass—

Each elementary particle has a fixed strength of coupling to the Higgs field. The stronger the coupling, the more the field holds it back, and the more mass it has. The top quark couples almost perfectly (a coupling of about 1); the electron barely at all (about 3 × 10⁻⁶). Plot mass against coupling and every particle falls on one straight line, which the LHC has now confirmed for the heavier ones.

So the field’s value is a dial for the universe. Turn it down and the electron gets lighter, so its orbit in an atom swells; atoms and everything built from them get bigger and floppier. Turn it to zero and electrons become massless: no atoms, no chemistry.

The proton barely notices. Most of its mass comes from the strong force (Φ 035), not the Higgs. Photons and gluons don’t couple at all, which is why they stay massless.

The proton mass shown is about 99% strong-force energy plus the scaled quark masses, ignoring the small knock-on change in the strong force. 03The discovery

Finding a bump in the data

Two-photon events—
Bump significance—
Verdict—

The Higgs boson is a ripple in the field, and it lives for only about 10⁻²² seconds. It can be detected only through what it decays into. One clean signature is a pair of high-energy photons: add up their energies and directions and you recover the mass of whatever made them.

The trouble is that ordinary collisions also make photon pairs, at all masses. The Higgs shows up only as a small bump on a large smooth background. With little data, random fluctuations make bumps everywhere. Particle physicists demand five standard deviations, a less than one-in-3.5-million chance of a fluke, before they claim a discovery.

On 4 July 2012, the ATLAS and CMS experiments at CERN announced a new particle at about 125 GeV, built from billions of collisions. Peter Higgs and François Englert, who predicted it in 1964, won the 2013 Nobel Prize.

Observations

The road to the Higgs

1964

Six theorists

Robert Brout and François Englert, Peter Higgs, and Gerald Guralnik, Carl Hagen and Tom Kibble independently showed how a field could give force carriers mass without wrecking the theory.

1967

Electroweak theory

Steven Weinberg and Abdus Salam used the mechanism to unify the electromagnetic and weak forces, predicting the W, Z and the Higgs boson.

2012

Discovery

ATLAS and CMS announced a Higgs-like boson at about 125 GeV. Later measurements confirmed spin 0 and couplings matching the Standard Model.

Open question

Is our vacuum stable?

With the measured Higgs and top masses, the Higgs field may not sit at its true lowest energy. If so, the universe is metastable, but the expected lifetime is vastly longer than its current age.

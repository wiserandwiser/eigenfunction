QUANTUM · teleportation — moving a quantum state to a distant particle without sending the particle

You can’t copy a quantum state, and you can’t measure it without destroying it. Yet with one shared entangled pair and two ordinary bits of information, the exact state can vanish from one place and reappear in another.

01The protocol

Five steps from here to there

Step—
Alice’s two bits—
Bob matches original—

Alice holds a qubit in some state, an arrow pointing anywhere on a sphere (pink). She wants Bob, far away, to have that exact state. She can’t just measure it and phone him the direction: one measurement gives only up or down and destroys the rest. And the no-cloning theorem forbids copying an unknown quantum state.

Here is the trick (Bennett and colleagues, 1993). Beforehand, Alice and Bob share an entangled pair. Notice that each half has no arrow of its own: a dot in the centre means “no state alone, only shared”. Alice then entangles her mystery qubit with her half of the pair and measures both. She gets two random bits.

At that instant, Bob’s qubit has become one of four simple variations of the original state. Which one depends on Alice’s two bits, so she phones him the bits (violet). He applies the matching correction, a flip or two, and his qubit is now exactly Alice’s original. Hers has been wiped in the process, so no copy was made.

After Alice’s CNOT and Hadamard, the three-qubit state splits into four equally likely branches. In each one Bob holds |ψ⟩ with a known Pauli error, so he undoes it with Zm₀Xm₁. Information Bob hasnone

Doesn’t this send something faster than light? The moment Alice measures, Bob’s qubit changes.

But it changes into one of four possibilities, each equally likely, and Bob doesn’t know which. Here are all four for the state you choose. Each is a flipped or rotated version of the original. Averaged over Alice’s random bits, the four arrows cancel exactly to the centre: a completely random qubit, whatever the original was.

Until the two ordinary bits arrive, at light speed or slower, Bob holds nothing useful. Teleportation moves a quantum state, not a message, and never faster than light. It is Φ 010’s no-signalling rule in action.

The math
¼ Σ σk |ψ⟩⟨ψ| σk = I/2

Averaging any state over the four Pauli operations I, X, Z, XZ gives the maximally mixed state. In Bloch vectors, (x, y, z), (x, −y, −z), (−x, −y, z) and (−x, y, −z) sum to zero.

03Real hardware

How good does the entanglement need to be?

Average fidelity—
Theory—
Better than classical?—

Real entangled pairs are imperfect. Photons lose quality crossing fibre or air, and qubits are disturbed by their surroundings. Model this as a pair that is perfect some fraction of the time and pure noise the rest.

The quality of teleportation is measured by fidelity: how closely Bob’s state matches the original, averaged over all possible states. Here is a sobering benchmark. With no entanglement at all, Alice could measure her qubit and phone the result, and Bob could rebuild a guess. That classical method scores ⅔ on average.

Teleportation only earns its name above ⅔, which needs a pair quality above ⅓. Each press runs the full simulation on 300 random states with noisy pairs and averages the results.

The first teleportation experiments came in 1997 in Innsbruck and Rome. In 2017 China’s Micius satellite teleported states from the ground to orbit, up to 1,400 km. In 2024 a team at Northwestern University teleported through 30 km of fibre while it also carried ordinary internet traffic.

The math
ρ = p|Φ+⟩⟨Φ+| + (1 − p) I/4  ⇒  F̄ = 1 + p2

For this Werner state the average teleportation fidelity rises in a straight line from ½ to 1. It beats the classical limit of ⅔ for p > ⅓. The simulation draws a perfect pair with probability p, and otherwise two independent random qubits, then runs the full protocol.

Observations

Where teleportation leads

Rule

No cloning

Wootters and Zurek proved in 1982 that no device can copy an unknown quantum state. Teleportation respects this: the original is destroyed as the copy appears. That same rule is what makes quantum cryptography (Φ 016) secure.

Networks

Quantum repeaters

Entanglement fades over long distances. Repeaters chain shorter entangled links using “entanglement swapping”, teleportation applied to entanglement itself, to span continents.

Computers

Moving qubits on a chip

Quantum computers use teleportation-style tricks to shuttle states between distant qubits, and to link separate quantum processors into one larger machine.

Science fiction

And people?

Teleporting a person would mean capturing the exact quantum state of about 10²⁸ atoms and destroying the original. The physics doesn’t forbid it in principle. The engineering is beyond any foreseeable technology.

Note to self

Shared entanglement plus two ordinary bits moves a quantum state intact. The original is destroyed, and nothing travels faster than the phone call.

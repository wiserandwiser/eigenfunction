ASTROPHYSICS · exoplanets — finding planets we can’t see, from a star’s faint dimming and tiny wobble

Until 1995 we knew of no planet around another Sun-like star. Now over 6,000 are confirmed, and statistics suggest most stars have planets. Almost none has been seen directly. They are found from the shadows they cast and the tugs they give their stars, effects as small as a flea crossing a car headlight.

01The transit

A planet crosses its star, and the light dips

Dip in brightness—
Transit lasts—
Odds our view lines up—

If a planet’s orbit happens to be edge-on to us, once per orbit it passes in front of its star and blocks a little light. The dip tells you the planet’s size: the fraction of light lost equals the fraction of the star’s disk covered.

Jupiter crossing the Sun would dim it by about 1%. Earth would dim it by under 0.01%, for about 13 hours, once a year. NASA’s Kepler telescope stared at 150,000 stars for four years to catch dips that small, finding about 2,700 planets. Its successor, TESS, surveys almost the whole sky.

The star is darker towards its edge, because there we see its cooler outer layers. That is why the bottom of each dip is curved, not flat.

Detectable today?—

A planet doesn’t orbit its star; both orbit their shared centre of mass. The star traces a tiny circle, moving towards us and then away. Its light is shifted very slightly bluer, then redder, and the dark lines in its spectrum slide back and forth.

In 1995 Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz found 51 Pegasi b this way: a planet half Jupiter’s mass orbiting its star every 4.2 days, closer than Mercury is to the Sun. Nobody had expected such “hot Jupiters”. The discovery won the 2019 Nobel Prize.

Earth makes the Sun wobble at only about 9 centimetres per second, slower than a tortoise. The best spectrographs today reach a few tens of centimetres per second, so finding a true Earth twin this way is still just out of reach.

K ≈ 28.4 m/s × (Mp/MJ) × (P/1 yr)−1/3,  Δλ/λ = v/c

For a Sun-like star and a circular, edge-on orbit; tilted orbits show only Mp sin i. The line shifts are exaggerated about a million times to be visible: 1 m/s shifts a line by about 2 millionths of a nanometre. Detection lines: HARPS (2003) about 1 m/s, ESPRESSO (2018) about 0.3 m/s.

03The habitable zone

Not too hot, not too cold

The habitable zone is the range of orbits where a rocky planet with an atmosphere could keep liquid water on its surface. It is not a guarantee of life, or even of water: Venus sits just inside the inner edge and Mars near the outer one.

Small red dwarfs, the commonest stars, are so dim that their habitable zones huddle very close, with “years” of days or weeks. The nearest exoplanet, Proxima b, orbits in its star’s zone every 11 days. Planets that close are probably tidally locked, one face in permanent day, and are blasted by their star’s flares.

The TRAPPIST-1 system, 40 light-years away, has seven Earth-sized planets, three or four of them in the zone. The James Webb Space Telescope is now testing whether any of them keeps an atmosphere at all.

Star brightness follows a simple mass–luminosity rule, and the preset stars use their measured values.

Worlds to know

1995 · 50 light-years

51 Pegasi b

The first planet found orbiting a Sun-like star: a hot Jupiter with a four-day year. It upended theories of how planetary systems form.

2016 · 4.2 light-years

Proxima b

A planet at least as massive as Earth in the habitable zone of the nearest star. Whether it keeps an atmosphere under its star’s flares is unknown.

2017 · 40 light-years

TRAPPIST-1

Seven Earth-sized planets around a star barely bigger than Jupiter, all closer to it than Mercury is to the Sun. JWST found that the innermost two have little or no thick atmosphere.

2022 · 700 light-years

WASP-39b

A hot, puffy gas giant whose starlight-filtered atmosphere gave JWST’s first clear detection of carbon dioxide on an exoplanet.

Note to self

We find other worlds by their shadows and tugs: a dip in starlight gives a planet’s size, a wobble gives its mass, and the star’s light sets whether water could be liquid.

SPACETIME ENGINEERING · warp — moving a patch of space, instead of moving a ship through space

Nothing can travel through space faster than light. But space itself has no speed limit: the universe is stretching faster than light right now. A warp drive is the idea of riding that loophole. The maths allows it. The price is a kind of energy that may not exist in the amounts needed.

01The loophole

Space can outrun light

Source recedes at—

Cosmic time—

The universe is expanding. Every distance between far-apart galaxies grows, and the farther away a galaxy is, the faster its distance from us grows.

Go far enough, about 14 billion light-years, and the gap grows faster than light. Past that light-speed line, galaxies are receding from us faster than light right now. No law is broken. Nothing is moving through space faster than light. The space in between is getting longer.

Watch a pulse of light sent toward us from a distant galaxy. It always moves at light speed through the space around it. But that space is being carried away from us. If it starts beyond the line, the light at first loses ground, like a swimmer in a current that is too fast for them.

That is the loophole. Light speed limits motion through space, but nothing limits how fast space itself can stretch or shrink. A warp drive asks: could we make space do that on purpose, in one small bubble?

Try thisSend light from just inside the line and watch how long it struggles before it gains ground. Then move the source beyond the line. In this universe that light never arrives.
The math
v = H D,  dDlightdt = H Dlight − c

Hubble’s law: recession speed grows in proportion to distance. The light-speed line (the Hubble radius) sits at c/H ≈ 14.4 billion light-years for H ≈ 68 km/s per megaparsec. A light pulse gains ground only where HD < c.

This model keeps H constant, a pure exponential expansion, which is roughly where our dark-energy-dominated universe is heading. Then light from beyond c/H never arrives, and the arrival time from inside is t = (c/H) ln[1/(1 − HD₀/c)]. In the real universe the rate has varied over time, so some light emitted from beyond the line does reach us. The true horizon for light sent today is about 16 billion light-years away.

In 1994 the physicist Miguel Alcubierre wrote down a shape of spacetime that does it. Picture a bubble around a ship. Ahead of the bubble, space shrinks. Behind it, space stretches. Inside, space is flat and calm.

The bubble slides forward like a wave carrying a surfer. The ship never moves through the space around it, so it never breaks the light-speed limit locally. The space around it does the moving, and that can be as fast as you like.

The surface shows the rate at which space is shrinking (ice, the dip ahead) or stretching (violet, the bump behind). It is the famous figure from Alcubierre’s paper, live. The grid lines are fixed marks in space sliding past the ship.

Strangest of all is life on board. The crew is in free fall: no acceleration, no crushing g-forces. Their clocks run at exactly the same rate as clocks back home. Unlike the twin paradox of near-light-speed rockets, nobody comes back younger.

The math
ds² = −c²dt² + (dx − vs f(rs) dt)² + dy² + dz²

The Alcubierre metric. vs is the bubble’s speed and f is a smooth step, 1 inside the bubble and 0 far outside:

f(r) = tanh(σ(r + R)) − tanh(σ(r − R))2 tanh(σR)

The surface plots the expansion of space, θ = vs (xs/rs) df/drs. It is negative ahead of the ship and positive behind. Inside the bubble f = 1, so a ship at rest there follows a geodesic with proper time equal to coordinate time: no time dilation, no felt acceleration.

Einstein’s equations work both ways. Choose a shape of spacetime and they tell you what matter and energy must be there to make it. For the warp bubble the answer is unsettling: the energy density must be negative, less than empty space. In the map, the brighter the violet, the more negative.

The negative energy sits in a doughnut-shaped ring around the ship’s waist. Push the speed up and it grows with the square of the speed. Sharpen the wall and it piles into a thinner, more intense ring.

Does negative energy exist? Surprisingly, yes, in tiny amounts. Put two metal plates very close together. Only waves that fit the gap can live between them, so the vacuum inside holds slightly less energy than the vacuum outside, and the plates are pushed together. This is the Casimir effect, measured precisely since 1997.

The trouble is scale. In 1997 Pfenning and Ford applied quantum rules that limit how negative energy can be concentrated. For a bubble 100 metres across, they found it would need roughly ten billion times the mass-energy of the entire visible universe. Later designs have cut that estimate dramatically, but none has removed the need for exotic energy at faster-than-light speeds.

Try thisClose the Casimir gap to 10 nanometres and read the pull: about one atmosphere. Then compare it with the map. Even this, the best negative energy we can make, is spread thin over a tiny gap.

Observations

The open problems

Happening now

Galaxies beyond the line

The most distant galaxies seen by the James Webb Space Telescope are now more than 30 billion light-years away and receding faster than light. We see them by light they sent long ago, when they were much closer.

Steering

Who flies the bubble?

In a faster-than-light bubble, the crew cannot send any signal to the front edge of the bubble, because that would mean outrunning light locally. So they could not steer it, start it or stop it from inside. The bubble would have to be set up in advance along the route.

Arrival

A blast at the destination

A 2012 study found that a warp bubble would sweep up particles along its path and release them when it stops. The arrival could hit the destination with a burst of high-energy radiation.

2021–2024

Warp fields with normal energy

Several recent papers built warp-like solutions that use only ordinary positive energy. They work only below light speed, but they suggest warp geometry is not all fantasy, and they turn it into an engineering question about mass and energy.

Note to self

Light speed limits how fast you move through space. Nothing limits how fast space itself can move.

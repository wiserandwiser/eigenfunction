BREAKTHROUGH TECHNOLOGY · energy storage — how lithium ions rock back and forth to power almost everything, and what comes next

The lithium-ion battery in your phone works by shuttling charged lithium atoms between two layered materials. Since its launch in 1991 its cost has fallen about 90% in just over a decade, turning electric cars and grid storage from dreams into everyday hardware. The next leap, solid-state cells, depends on taming lithium’s habit of growing needles.

01The rocking chair

Ions one way inside, electrons the other way outside

State of charge—
Voltage—
A full charge-or-empty takes—

A lithium-ion cell has two electrodes that can each store lithium between their atomic layers, like books slotted onto shelves. On one side is graphite, stacked sheets of carbon. On the other is a layered metal oxide, often of nickel, manganese and cobalt (NMC), or iron phosphate (LFP).

When you use the battery, lithium atoms in the graphite give up an electron each. The ions travel through the liquid electrolyte and a thin porous separator to the oxide. The electrons can’t pass through the electrolyte, so they take the long way round, through your phone, doing work on the way. Charging pushes both back. Nothing is burned or used up; the lithium just rocks back and forth, which is why it is called a rocking-chair battery.

Pull current faster and the voltage sags, because the cell’s internal resistance turns some energy into heat. That is why fast charging gets warm, and why chargers slow down near full.

LixC₆ + Li1−xMO₂ ⇄ C₆ + LiMO₂,  V = VOC(SoC) − IR

Current is shown as a C-rate: 1C empties a full cell in one hour, 2C in thirty minutes. The open-circuit voltage curve is a smoothed NMC-graphite shape, 3.0–4.2 V, and the internal resistance is a single fixed value. Real cells also have slower diffusion effects, and their voltage depends on temperature and age.

02Energy density

Build an electric car’s battery

Pack energy—
Pack mass—
Pack volume—

What matters for a car is energy density: how much energy a kilogram, or a litre, of battery holds. A typical electric car uses about 16 kWh per 100 km, so range comes down to how much battery you can carry.

Chemistries trade off. NMC packs the most energy but uses costly nickel and cobalt. LFP (lithium iron phosphate) holds less but is cheaper, safer and lasts thousands of cycles, and now powers most new electric cars sold in China. Sodium-ion avoids lithium altogether, using abundant salt, at a further cost in density.

Petrol holds about 12,000 Wh per kilogram, around fifty times more than the best batteries. But an engine wastes most of it as heat, while an electric motor uses about 90%. The gap that actually matters is closer to fifteen-fold, and shrinking.

The math
pack energy = range × 0.16 kWh/km,  mass = energy / (Wh/kg),  volume = energy / (Wh/L)

Rough pack-level figures, including casing and cooling, about 2025: lead–acid 30 Wh/kg and 50 Wh/L; sodium-ion 110 and 150; LFP 140 and 220; NMC 190 and 300; solid-state targets around 300 and 500. Cells alone are denser still. The pack is drawn as a slab 2.4 m × 1.4 m under the floor; real packs add structure and cooling. Real consumption varies with speed, size and weather.

03The next leap

Taming lithium’s needles

Status—
Lithium plated—

The biggest prize is to swap graphite for pure lithium metal, which stores about ten times more per gram. The trouble is how lithium plates back on when you charge. Atoms land wherever they first touch, and any bump sticks out further into the flow of ions, so it catches more and grows faster.

The result is dendrites: branching needles of lithium that can pierce the separator and short-circuit the cell, sometimes starting a fire. Fast charging makes it worse. This is why lithium-metal anodes, first tried in the 1970s, were abandoned for graphite.

Solid-state batteries replace the flammable liquid with a solid ceramic or polymer electrolyte that is meant to press lithium flat and block needles. Getting a solid to stay in perfect contact through thousands of charges is hard; carmakers are now targeting the late 2020s.

Try thisCharge with a liquid electrolyte, fast, and watch the needles race across. Then switch to solid and compare.
The math
diffusion-limited aggregation:  random walk + drift → stick on contact

A teaching model, not an electrochemical simulation. Each ion takes a random walk with a drift towards the electrode; faster charging means stronger drift and less time to spread out. In “liquid” mode ions stick wherever they first touch; in “solid” mode sharp tips are disfavoured, mimicking the mechanical pressure and even current of a good solid electrolyte. Real solid electrolytes can still grow dendrites along grain boundaries.

Observations

Milestones

1970s–1985

The inventors

Stanley Whittingham made the first lithium-intercalation battery, John Goodenough found the cobalt-oxide cathode, and Akira Yoshino built the safe graphite design. They shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

1991

Sony’s first cell

The first commercial lithium-ion battery shipped in a Sony camcorder. Laptops and mobile phones followed, then electric cars.

Prices

The cost collapse

Average pack prices fell from over $1,000 per kWh in 2010 to about $115 in 2024, driven by scale, much as with solar panels (Φ 029).

The grid

Grid batteries

Huge battery parks now store midday solar for the evening peak. In California, on many days batteries now supply the largest share of power in the early evening.
